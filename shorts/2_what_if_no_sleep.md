# What If You Never Slept Again?
Facts: Randy Gardner stayed awake 264 h (1964), hallucinations, memory loss, slurred speech; microsleeps; fatal familial insomnia is real, rare, deadly; rats deprived of sleep die in ~2-3 weeks; brain clears waste during sleep.

What if you just never slept again?

Day one. You're a bit groggy. Coffee fixes it. Barely.

Day two. Your focus slips. You blink, and three seconds are gone. Your brain is taking tiny naps without asking. Scientists call them microsleeps.

Day three. Words get slippery. You forget what you were saying. Mood swings hit hard.

By day four, things move at the edge of your eyes. Shadows with faces. Your brain is making things up.

In 1964, a teenager named Randy Gardner stayed awake for eleven days. He slurred, forgot, and saw things that weren't there. Then he slept, and he recovered.

But he stopped. If you can't stop, trouble starts.

Sleep is when your brain flushes out waste. Without it, junk builds up.

In rats, total sleep loss kills in a few weeks. A rare human disease does the same.

So tonight? Go to bed. Your brain has chores to do.
