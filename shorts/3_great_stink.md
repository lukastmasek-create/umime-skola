# The Great Stink (London, 1858)
Facts: summer 1858 heat; Thames full of sewage; Parliament curtains soaked in lime/chloride; MPs considered leaving London; Bazalgette's sewer system approved in 18 days; ~1,100 miles of sewers, 318M bricks; cholera dropped.

In 1858, London smelled so bad that Parliament almost ran away.

A hot summer. A river full of sewage. A city of three million people. Everything flowed into the Thames.

Then the sun hit. The smell crept across the water, through the windows, into the halls of power.

Lawmakers tried everything. They soaked the curtains in chemicals. They held handkerchiefs to their faces. They kept fleeing the room.

For years, doctors had blamed bad air for cholera. So the stink felt like proof. Doom was coming.

Finally, Parliament acted. In just eighteen days, they approved a plan from engineer Joseph Bazalgette.

He dug eleven hundred miles of sewers. Three hundred million bricks.

The waste went downstream, far away from town. Cholera dropped. Those sewers still work today.

Moral: great cities fall for a stink. Great cities also fix it.
